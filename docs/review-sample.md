# The Page Under the Page

This page was written badly on purpose. It reads well enough, I think, but it is the kind of first draft that most of us basically produce on any given day. That is fine. A first draft only has to exist. The second one has to be read.

Open Review and the page starts to talk back. A sentence that runs on for too long turns yellow, and one that runs on for much too long turns red, like this one, which keeps adding clauses because the writer was afraid to stop, and which, by the time it reaches its end, has asked the reader to hold far more in mind than any reader should have to. Smaller marks sit on single words: blue for the ones that weaken a sentence, green for the ones with a plainer twin. None of them change anything. They point, and then they wait.

Some of the words they point at are easy to spot. "Very" is very rarely needed, and "really" almost never is. Others hide better. We make a decision when we could decide, and we give consideration to an idea when we could simply consider it. The verb is still in there, buried inside a noun.

Negatives are sneakier still. When a sentence says it is not possible to do something, the reader has to picture the thing and then take it away. Say what is, not what isn't.

Then there are the long words we utilize because they feel more serious. Numerous writers commence a paragraph with a long windup in order to sound thorough, when a plain start would do the same work.

The editor does not ask you to fix all of this at once. A word you might cut can be ghosted: it stays on the page, faint, and you read the sentence without it before you decide. A word you are not sure of can keep its alternatives, and you switch between them in place. A paragraph can keep each version you write of it underneath, so nothing you try is lost.

Some mistakes are just mistakes, though. Its easy to loose track of a typo after you have read the same line ten times, and the words that slips past the eye are definately the ones a reader will notice. Ask for a check, and they will be marked in pink.

So read the page one more time. Let the marks speak quietly, and decide for yourself which ones to listen to.
